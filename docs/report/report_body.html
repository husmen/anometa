TabPFN-3.5 hackathon · few-shot industrial anomaly detection

Anometa on MVTec AD 2

Can TabPFN-3.5 turn a handful of labelled defect images into a useful defect detector, using only frozen features from a vision foundation model? We tested this on the 8 scenarios of MVTec AD 2, against supervised and label-free controls and against standard unsupervised baselines, with a protocol that evaluates the final configuration once on held-out data.

1Summary

  • TabPFN-3.5 beats logistic regression with one labelled defect: lock AUROC {tab1} against {log1}, a paired difference of {d1}. At k = 2 and k = 5 it leads by about 0.04 ({tab2} vs {log2}, {tab5} vs {log5}), but those intervals include zero. With three budgets tested, the k = 1 result does not survive a Bonferroni correction on its own.
  • TabPFN is much better calibrated. Its probabilities, corrected to a 50/50 prior, have about half the negative log-likelihood of logistic regression at every k. Logistic regression only matches it after its regularisation is tuned with labels that a few-shot user does not have.
  • TabPFN ties the label-free controls. A Mahalanobis distance ({mah}) and TabPFN's own unsupervised outlier score ({tout}) on the same features need no labels and are statistically level with TabPFN at k = 1 to 5. Logistic regression and kNN fall below them. On AD2, a few labels add little over a good novelty score, and TabPFN is the only supervised model here that keeps up.
  • The foundation-model features do most of the work. The training-free DINOv3-L patch distance map ties the best of our pixel-level methods (AU-PRO@0.05 {dl_pro}; PatchCore {pc_pro} at its 256×256 default, {pc512_pro} when tuned to 512×512 on dev; EfficientAD-S {ead_pro}), and its image AUROC ({dl_auc}) is close to TabPFN's at k = 5. Both best maps are above the AD2 paper's best mean, mostly thanks to higher input resolution, and the test sets differ (section 6). This shows promise, not a ranking.
  • TabPFN-3.5-Fast matches TabPFN-3.5 within 0.003 AUROC at every k, at about a third of the prediction time.
  • Post-freeze studies (section 8). TabPFN-3.5-Thinking matches local TabPFN-3.5 (AUROC +0.000 at k = 2) at about 200,000 API tokens per fit. Giving TabPFN a few good images under a new light improves its calibration there but not its ranking, because with DINOv3-L features the lighting gap is already only about 0.01 AUROC. A prototype that scores one row per patch gives TabPFN anomaly maps that beat the DINOv3 distance map by 0.06 to 0.07 AU-PRO@0.05 over all 8 scenarios (dev split), and beat logistic regression on the same rows.
  • The lock split changed the story. On the dev split TabPFN and logistic regression looked level, with one scenario (wall plugs) favouring logistic regression. On the untouched lock split that scenario reversed. Small per-scenario differences on AD2 are within scene-sampling noise.

2Data and protocol

MVTec AD 2 [3] has 8 industrial scenarios. Each has defect-free training and validation images, a labelled public test set, and private test sets that only MVTec's server scores. The public test images show every scene under regular lighting and under 3 to 6 changed lighting conditions (over- and under-exposure, extra spotlights), which the training images never show. The archives come from MVTec's official download page [4] and are never redistributed.

{table_dataset}

Dev and lock halves

The private test sets need pixel anomaly maps for scoring, and the few-shot method needs labelled defects. So the project splits the public test set once per scenario, by scene, into two halves. All lighting variants of one scene stay in the same half, so the same defect never appears in both. The dev half is for every experiment and every model choice. The lock half is evaluated once, after the final configuration is committed.

3Models

The project has two tracks. Track B is the subject: frozen image features, a short table per image, and a few-shot classifier. Track A holds the standard unsupervised baselines, which train on normal images only and produce pixel anomaly maps.

Frozen vision encoders

Three vision transformers [14] were tested: DINOv3 [6] ViT-S/16 and ViT-L/16, and SigLIP2 [7] so400m/16 (NaFlex). DINOv3 adds 4 register tokens [15] next to the CLS token. None of the encoders is fine-tuned. DINOv3-L was best for every classifier on the dev split and is used in the final configuration. Encoders run in bfloat16 on CUDA, because float16 overflows in DINOv3-L.

From an image to one table row

Each image becomes one short row of numbers. The CLS token and the mean of all patch tokens describe the whole image. A PCA, fitted on the training normals only, compresses them to 16 to 128 values. Two novelty values describe local defects: each patch of the image gets the distance to its nearest patch among all training normals, and the largest of these distances and the mean of the top 1% are kept. Training images get leave-one-image-out novelty, so that normal training rows and normal test rows are scored the same way. The final configuration uses DINOv3-L, CLS + mean patch with PCA 16, plus the 2 novelty values: 18 columns.

TabPFN-3.5

TabPFN [1, 8, 9] is a transformer pre-trained on millions of synthetic tables to do supervised learning in context. At use time it receives the labelled rows and the test rows together and outputs class probabilities in one forward pass, with no gradient updates. Its layers alternate attention across the features of a row and attention across rows, where test rows read the labelled context rows [8]. TabPFN on frozen embeddings of images and text has been shown to be accurate and well calibrated [2]; this project tests that idea on few-shot industrial anomaly detection. Here the context is about 300 normal rows (label 0) and k defect rows (label 1) per scenario.

Variants used:

  • TabPFN-3.5 and TabPFN-3.5-Fast [1], local weights, default settings in the final configuration. Each process loads a checkpoint once and reuses it, which cut a fit from 911 ms to 11 ms.
  • TabPFN outlier score (tabpfn-extensions, unsupervised): it estimates how likely a row is under the normal rows by the chain rule, with TabPFN regressors that predict each feature from the features before it, averaged over random feature orders. It uses no defect labels.
  • Context size (n_normals: 32): the same classifier with only 32 normal rows in its context. Added after a dev probe; tested once on lock.
  • TabPFN-3.5-Thinking (API, with a disk cache) ran only as a post-freeze ablation at k = 2 (section 8); it is not part of the lock results.

Controls

  • Logistic regression on standardised features, default C = 1. Same rows, same shots.
  • kNN on standardised features, 5 neighbours.
  • Mahalanobis distance to the normal rows [13], with a Ledoit-Wolf covariance [22]. It uses no labels.

Track A: unsupervised baselines with anomaly maps

PatchCore [10] and EfficientAD-S [11] come from anomalib [5] with the AD2 paper's stated settings [3]: 256×256 input, PatchCore with a WideResNet-50 [16] backbone and a 1% coreset, EfficientAD-S trained for 70,000 steps with Imagenette as its penalty set, instead of the ImageNet that the original method uses. These are the frozen settings. After the freeze, a declared dev-split sweep tuned PatchCore's input size and coreset ratio; the selected 512×512 setting was then evaluated once on lock as a separate row. EfficientAD-S was not tuned. The DINOv3 patch distance map needs no training: it reuses the patch distances already computed for the novelty features.

4Testing framework

Every experiment goes through one function, run_experiment(config) → result, called by the command line, the search tools and the demo. A run writes artifacts/<name>-<config hash>/ with a manifest (git commit, dataset and split hashes, model revisions, licences, hardware, versions), the metrics and every per-image prediction. Interrupted runs resume; complete dev runs are reused; lock runs can never run twice.

Seeds and shots

  • Each Track B configuration runs 10 seeds per scenario. A seed draws the k shots from the regular-lit dev defect images. Draws are nested: the k = 1 shot is the first of the k = 5 shots for the same seed.
  • Two classifiers at the same k and seed see exactly the same shots and are scored on exactly the same images, so their results can be compared pair by pair.

Metrics

  • Image level: AUROC (main metric) and AUPRC. Calibration: expected calibration error (ECE) [17], negative log-likelihood (NLL) and Brier score, each also after correcting the probabilities to a 50/50 prior [18] (the balanced versions).
  • Robustness gap: the metric on regular-lit images minus the same metric on shifted lighting.
  • Pixel level (Track A): AU-PRO up to a false-positive rate of 0.05 and 0.30 (the area under the per-region overlap curve, normalised) [12, 3], SegF1 and ClassF1 at one threshold per scenario: the mean plus 3 standard deviations of the defect-free validation maps [3].

Uncertainty

Searching on dev

  • Grid: 3 encoders × 2 feature sets × 4 PCA sizes × 4 classifiers × 3 values of k, plus a novelty-only ablation and the label-free controls (366 runs).
  • Optuna [19] TPE [20] and a TabPFN-surrogate Bayesian optimiser (TabPFN regression predicts the AUROC of untried configurations), 60 trials, 3 seeds each.
  • NSGA-II [21], 200 trials, AUROC against balanced ECE.

Guardrails against leakage

  • No feature, hyperparameter or split choice uses lock results. Searches never see lock labels.
  • The order is fixed: dev experiments, then a commit that freezes the configurations, then one lock run.
  • Every attempt, including failed ones, is appended to runs.jsonl.

  • Our two best maps tie on the mean: the training-free DINOv3-L distance map and PatchCore at 512×512, tuned on the dev split after the freeze (38.5 each). Between them they beat the paper's best method on 6 of 8 scenarios and lose on Can and Fruit Jelly. 7What the dev split showed

Searches converge on one basin

Dev against lock

The dev half evaluates only the scenes left after the shots are drawn; the lock half evaluates all of its scenes. With 7 to 8 defect scenes per half and scenario, scenario-level AUROC moves by 0.1 to 0.2 between halves.

On dev, TabPFN's scores followed a distance-to-normal score closely (Spearman 0.57 to 0.91 against Mahalanobis), and on Wall Plugs that pattern cost it. On lock, Wall Plugs favoured TabPFN (0.61 to 0.66 against 0.43 to 0.48). The lock split did its job: a dev-only story did not survive.

Tried and dropped

  • A rank average of TabPFN and logistic regression: no gain on dev.
  • Larger TabPFN ensembles (n_estimators): scores barely changed.
  • 32 normals in context: better dev calibration, but no gain on lock.

8Post-freeze studies

Two experiments run after the single lock evaluation. They are not part of the lock benchmark, and neither changes the frozen configuration.

Can TabPFN adapt to new lighting in context?

A factory changes its lighting. Photographing a few good parts under the new light is cheap; labelling defects is not. The question: if m such images join TabPFN's context, does detection under that light improve, without any retraining? The protocol was committed before any run. Whole scenes rotate between context and evaluation over every public test scene (10 folds per scenario, dev and lock halves together), so no scene is ever both in the context and scored, under any lighting. Everything else is the frozen configuration.

{table_lighting}
  • The primary hypothesis is not supported: two adaptation scenes change TabPFN's AUROC under shifted lighting by +0.002, within noise. There is little to recover: with DINOv3-L features the lighting gap is only about 0.01 AUROC for TabPFN (0.735 regular, 0.727 shifted) and zero for logistic regression.
  • Calibration does improve: balanced NLL under shifted lighting drops by 0.05 (k = 2) to 0.065 (k = 1), a clear and consistent gain.
  • Under shifted light, TabPFN beats Mahalanobis by 0.045 AUROC and leads logistic regression by 0.015 to 0.025 (intervals touch zero), with far better calibration.
  • The hardest conditions for TabPFN are shift_3 (0.701) and shift_1 (0.710), against 0.735 under regular light.
  • The six tabpfn_outlier runs (18 features, one regressor refit per feature) took 4 to 15 hours each. They show no lighting gap either (shifted AUROC 0.709 to 0.713), and TabPFN-3.5 leads the outlier score at m = 2 by +0.016 (k = 2) and +0.012 (k = 1); both intervals include zero.

TabPFN maps from one row per patch (prototype)

Instead of one row per image, each DINOv3 patch (16×16 pixels at a 512-pixel short side, about 1,300 per image) becomes one row: PCA-32 of its patch token, its DINOv3 nearest-patch distance, the PatchCore map averaged over the patch, and the 3×3 maxima of both maps. Defect masks label the patches of the k shot images; half of the validation images give the normal rows. TabPFN scores every patch of every evaluation image, and the scores form an anomaly map. Rows are patches, not pixels: a 2448×2048 image has 5 million pixels but the features only exist per patch, so the map is upsampled like Track A's. Dev split only, all 8 scenarios, 3 seeds, k = 2 and 5. Intervals resample (scenario, seed) units.

{table_trackc}
  • TabPFN learns a better map than its inputs. Over all 8 scenarios, TabPFN-Fast's map beats the DINOv3 distance map by 0.056 (k = 2) and 0.071 (k = 5) AU-PRO@0.05, the unsupervised fusion by 0.09 to 0.12, and logistic regression on the same rows by 0.02 to 0.07; every interval excludes zero. It is the strongest TabPFN-specific result of the project.
  • With PatchCore tuned to 512×512 (post-freeze rerun), TabPFN-Fast's map barely changes (0.452 and 0.470 at k = 2 and 5). It still beats the distance map (+0.059, +0.073) and the tuned PatchCore (+0.12, +0.17). The label-free fusion of the two tuned maps gets much stronger: it ties TabPFN-Fast at k = 2 (+0.018, interval includes zero), while TabPFN leads at k = 5 (+0.057).
  • Largest gains at k = 5: Wall Plugs 0.006 → 0.20, Sheet Metal 0.55 → 0.72, Walnuts 0.50 → 0.64. Vial, saturated for the DINOv3 distance (0.87), is the one clear loss (0.83).
  • Caveats: dev split only, 3 seeds; the patch labelling rule was changed once, after a crash on Can (its defects are smaller than 30% of any patch), not after looking at scores. Confirming it would need its own frozen protocol and held-out data.
  • Cost on the 3090: 15 to 30 s per scenario with TabPFN-3.5-Fast for 100,000 to 250,000 scored patches and a context of about 18,000 rows.
  • A threshold rule made for distances (validation mean + 3 standard deviations) suits probability maps poorly, so SegF1 often drops; a probability threshold is the next step.

Same results on every machine

All 20 frozen Track B configurations were rerun on the dev split on the M4 Pro (MPS) and on the Ryzen 7 7700 with the GPU hidden, from the same cached features. Logistic regression, kNN and Mahalanobis give identical scores everywhere. TabPFN's scores move by at most 0.005 AUROC on the M4 Pro and 0.001 on the CPU, and every interval includes zero.

10Limitations

  • Lock numbers use half of AD2's public test set, so they are not directly comparable with published AD2 results, which use the private test sets (see section 6).
  • EfficientAD-S runs with fixed default settings. PatchCore was tuned only for input size and coreset ratio, on the dev split after the freeze; larger inputs than 512×512 were not tried.
  • Lock sets are small: 7 defect scenes and 2 to 6 good scenes per scenario, each under 4 to 7 lighting conditions. Intervals are wide.
  • The lock evaluation covers TabPFN image scores only. TabPFN anomaly maps exist only as a dev-split prototype (section 8) without a frozen protocol or lock evaluation.
  • Track A runs at 256×256 (as in the AD2 paper), except the tuned PatchCore at 512×512; Track B encodes images at a 512-pixel short side. Image-level comparisons across tracks mix these settings.
  • SegF1 is pooled over all images of a scenario, which is our reading of the metric.
