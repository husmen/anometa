Abstract

Factories need to catch defects, but defects are rare: often there is a handful of labelled examples, sometimes one. Can TabPFN-3.5, a foundation model for tables, turn a few labelled defect images into a useful detector, using only frozen features from a vision foundation model? Anometa encodes each image with a frozen DINOv3 vision transformer, compresses it to one table row, and lets TabPFN-3.5 classify new rows in context, with no gradient updates. On the 8 scenarios of MVTec AD 2, under a protocol that freezes the configuration on a dev split and evaluates it once on a held-out lock split, TabPFN reaches a lock AUROC of 0.761 with one labelled defect per scenario (logistic regression: 0.683), and its probabilities have about half the balanced log loss. A good label-free novelty score ranks images just as well, so on this benchmark the frozen DINOv3 features do most of the work. A prototype that scores one row per image patch turns TabPFN into a defect-map generator that beats its own input maps on the dev split.

Why "Anometa"?

Anomaly + meta. Today, "meta" means meta-learning: TabPFN was pre-trained on millions of synthetic tasks and learns a new one in context. Tomorrow, it means a meta-framework: the experiment API, artifacts and protocol are not tied to one model or dataset, so other foundation models, detectors and benchmarks can plug in. TabPFN-3.5 and MVTec AD 2 are the starting point.

Method

One image becomes one table row: the DINOv3 global token and mean patch (compressed by PCA) plus two novelty numbers, the distances of the image's most unusual patches to their nearest normal patch.

TabPFN reads the rows of a few hundred normal images and k labelled defects as its context and predicts, in one forward pass, the probability that each new image is defective. There is no training and no tuning: fitting takes milliseconds.

The training-free DINOv3-L distance map has the highest mean but wins on only 4 of 8 scenarios. Our PatchCore and EfficientAD-S run untuned with anomalib defaults and score below the paper's numbers for the same methods, and the test sets differ. This shows promise, not a ranking: a conclusive comparison needs tuned baselines, a tuned pipeline of our own, and a submission to MVTec's evaluation server.

TabPFN as a defect-map generator (prototype)

Each 16×16-pixel patch becomes a row (patch token, DINOv3 distance, PatchCore value and their 3×3 maxima), and TabPFN-3.5-Fast scores every patch. On the dev split of all 8 scenarios its maps beat the DINOv3 distance map by +0.056 (k = 2) and +0.071 (k = 5) AU-PRO@0.05. Dev only, 3 seeds, no frozen protocol yet.

Speed and reproducibility

  • TabPFN fits in about 5 ms; one scenario runs end to end in about 14 s on an RTX 3090 (2.1 min on an Apple M4 Pro, 9.8 min on a CPU).
  • Results match within 0.005 AUROC on the M4 Pro and within 0.001 on a CPU-only Ryzen.
  • Every run writes a manifest (git commit, dataset, split and config hashes, model revisions, licences, seeds, hardware), metrics and per-image predictions.

Limitations

  • Lock numbers use half of the public test set (7 defect scenes per scenario), so intervals are wide and the numbers are not directly comparable with the private test set.
  • The baselines and our pipeline were not tuned for pixel metrics.
  • TabPFN maps exist only as a dev-split prototype.
